# Learner Profile

## Initial Idea
Their idea in their own terms, or that they want help finding one. Include intended users or motivation only if shared. This is a starting point, not approved scope.
> FocusFlow: a personal focus and goal-alignment tool for students and self-learners. A learner chooses one daily task that supports a meaningful long-term goal, focuses on it, and reflects on what happened. The app offers a simple, editable connection sentence rather than deciding whether the goal is right for them.

## Demonstrated Technical and Agent Experience
Their reported coding background and AI coding-agent experience, distinguished from what you directly observed. Include tools or workflows only if mentioned; don't require a build history.
Evidence: a short quote or paraphrase.
> They know HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Express.js, and MongoDB. They have used Codex to generate project code, then reviewed, tested, and tried to understand how the components fit together. They said they are still improving their debugging and independent coding ability.

## Planning-Workflow Experience
Reported experience with planning first or spec-driven development, separate from coding seniority: new, familiar, or not established, with brief evidence in their words. No terminology test or résumé required.
> New to a consistent, structured plan-first workflow. They have done some planning before but not regularly used separate scope, requirements, and technical specification documents.

## Desired Learning Outcome
One practical learning intention in their words, if known: a tool, an unfamiliar concept, or a reusable way of working with an agent. "Not established" is fine; getting something working is valid. Don't invent a capability target or keep asking until they name one.
> They want to learn how to plan before coding, use Codex effectively, understand generated code, and test the final result. They also want to learn the process of building with an AI agent in a way that is maintainable and understandable.

## Learning Moments
Optional, brief notes about an actual question, decision, or practice relevant to the intention: what happened and where the evidence lives (a document heading, test, or code path). Record only useful moments as they occur; not a log after every exchange. Distinguish an agent explanation from something the learner actually tried or articulated. Don't claim mastery.
> Not established yet.

## Interests, Inspirations, and Goals
Useful context they chose to share: interests, examples they admire, how the idea fits their life, or who they hope will use it. Summarize only what helps shape the project; omit unnecessary personal details.
> They are interested in practical software that solves real organization and time-management problems. They want a useful but simple project, not an overly complex app. They are motivated by learning how to plan, work with an AI coding agent, and understand code they did not write from scratch.

## Vocabulary and Concepts Likely to Need Explanation
Concepts their stated background or expressed uncertainty suggests need explaining, and terms they clearly understand. Not mentioning a term is not evidence they don't know it.
> Likely to need explanation: plan-first workflow, scope, PRD, technical spec, prioritization logic, task urgency vs. impact, basic algorithmic decision-making, and testing for a small full-stack app. They clearly understand HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Express.js, and MongoDB.

## Collaboration and Communication Preferences
Only preferences they volunteered or explicitly confirmed: concise or guided explanations; one-at-a-time or small batches of related questions. Unset means guided, one at a time. No additional preferences questionnaire, and no inference from seniority.
> Guided explanations and one-at-a-time questions are preferred. They also prefer starting with familiar technologies and only adding complexity if needed.

## Areas Where the Learner Wants Ownership
Decisions they explicitly said they want to make themselves, if any. Unknown preferences never waive approval requirements.
> They want to understand the reasoning behind the implementation and maintain the project themselves. They prefer to learn step by step and want to be asked before major decisions are made.

## Likely Support Needs
Help they requested or friction grounded in their reported background or observed uncertainty. Distinguish known needs from open questions.
> They want help with plan-first workflow habits, debugging problems, and understanding AI-generated code. They also want a small project that is testable and maintainable rather than large or overly polished.

Anything relevant that doesn't fit above, and unknowns a later phase may need to ask about when they matter. Optional unanswered questions are not tasks to complete.
> Need to keep the project focused on a small proof of concept. Future skills can decide whether the app is single-page or simple Express + MongoDB backend depending on the chosen scope and complexity.
